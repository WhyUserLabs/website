Six evidence sources compile into your buyer agents. Three are public and work from the first run: company context from your site, ACV and GTM motion; market intelligence from reviews, communities and competitors; and AI awareness of how ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity describe you. Three are yours and start when you connect them: customer voice from call recordings and CRM objections; institutional knowledge from win/loss notes, positioning and battlecards; and expert knowledge from experiment findings and the corrections your team makes. The agents recompile as your data changes.

From personas to a committee

Five roles.
Three moods.

A B2B deal needs more than one yes. WhyUser groups buyers into the committee that decides, then reads your page as each role, in the mood a real reader brings.

Your page

    Can I sell this upstairs?Proof they can forward to the people who sign.

  • End user

    Will it fit my day?The daily work, shown getting easier.

  • Technical lead

    Will it work with ours?Integrations, setup time and architecture.

  • Budget owner

    What will it cost us?How pricing works and a return they can defend.

  • Security

    Is our data safe?Data flow, compliance documents and controls.

Each role reads in three moods

The mix follows the channel. A cold LinkedIn ad brings mostly distracted readers. A search visitor is mostly skeptical or ideal.

Common questions

Answers, briefly.

What is a WhyUser buyer persona?

A simulated buyer compiled from real evidence: reviews, community threads, buyer questions, competitor complaints and, when you connect them, your sales calls, CRM objections and win/loss notes. Every trait cites its source. The buyer is simulated, not a real person.

What is Buyer Context?

The evidence behind your buyers: what they say in public, what they say about your rivals, what they say on your calls, and which concerns are getting louder or going quiet. Every quote is dated, and every finding shows how many sources carry it.

How is a buyer persona different from an ICP?

An ICP describes the company you sell to: industry, size and stack. A persona describes a person on its buying committee: what they need, fear and must prove. WhyUser covers both. Audience Discovery finds the titles and sub-markets your content fits, and personas read the content as those people.

Can I build personas from my own customer interviews and sales calls?

Yes. Connect call transcripts, or paste a document or URL with your research on a buyer. WhyUser pulls out the statements, pins them to that buyer, and shows you anything it refused, such as your own marketing. Public evidence works before you connect anything.

Can a persona predict buyer objections, not just describe a buyer?

Yes. Each persona carries the objections and proof needs its role is likely to raise, then reads your real page: which role stalls, on which line, and what proof was missing. One account confirmed 6 of 8 predicted proof needs on 42 recorded sales calls, and 2 more in part.

Do the personas change over time?

Only if you want them to. Leave a buyer live and it rebuilds when new evidence lands. Freeze it to hold a fixed baseline for a test. It never drifts on its own.

Are my call transcripts safe?

Transcripts are redacted in memory on the way in, so personal data is never written to disk. Data is encrypted, hosted on AWS in US regions, and not used to train base models. Details on the security page.
