The gap

You test with the 3% and ship to the 97%.

Three dials decide how a page reads: motivation, product knowledge, attention. Your reviewers are maxed on all three. Your buyers are not.

Internal review sees

  • One seat. Marketing, reviewing a five-seat decision.
  • Maximum motivation. Their name is on it.
  • Full product knowledge. Nobody present can un-know it.
  • Full attention. A blocked meeting with the page on screen.

WhyUser sees

  • Five seats. Champion, economic buyer, tech owner, end user, guardian.
  • The full range. All three dials, low to high, weighted to the channel you buy.
  • Cold arrival. Every agent lands from a forwarded link.
  • Seven handoffs. Scored healthy, at risk or broken.

Why it happens

Three failures a review cannot catch.

One seat

No economic buyer in the room.

Nobody asks what the current alternative costs, or where the security proof is.

No handoff

The most expensive failure is between roles.

Arm your champion with enthusiasm but no number and it stalls, while every reader still approves the page.

Ties break on rank

The most senior person wins.

A fine way to run a company. A poor way to test a message, because seniority tracks distance from the buyer.

Free

Five minutes that improve your next review.

Costs nothing and does not require us.

Before discussion starts

Everyone privately writes down which committee role would refuse this page, and what that role would need to see.

Then read them out

If everyone names the same role, you are all modelling one buyer. If nobody can answer, the review is not testing what you think it is.

Common questions

Answers, briefly.

Is internal review enough before launch?

Enough for typos, brand, legal risk and product accuracy. Not for why a stranger leaves.

Does this replace internal review?

No. Run the stress test first, then spend the two weeks on what only your team can judge.

What is the handoff failure?

Deals move when one role carries evidence to the next. If the page arms nobody, every role can still approve.
