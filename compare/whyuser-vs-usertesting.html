WhyUser vs. UserTesting

UserTesting shows you a person struggling with your interface. WhyUser shows you a buying committee deciding against your offer. Both matter. They fail in different places, and confusing the two wastes a quarter.

The short version

UserTesting is an enterprise human insight platform. Real participants complete tasks on your site while narrating what they are doing, and you get recorded sessions, surveys, and analysis back. It is built for user experience research: where people get stuck, what confuses them, which flows break.

WhyUser is a pre-launch stress test for the buying decision. It simulates each seat on the B2B buying committee reading a page, ad, or email, and reports which role abandons, at which section, and what proof was missing.

The distinction is not human versus simulated. It is can they use it versus will they buy it.

The failure each one catches

A B2B landing page can fail in two completely different ways, and teams routinely diagnose one while suffering from the other.

Usability failure

The form has eleven fields, the CTA is below three screens of copy, and the pricing link opens a modal that traps focus. A participant gives up. UserTesting will show you this in ten minutes of video, and it is worth fixing.

Decision failure

The page is fast, clear, and beautifully built. The champion loves it. It is forwarded to the security reviewer, who finds no SOC 2 signal and no data-residency statement, says nothing, and the thread goes quiet. No usability study will find this, because nothing about the experience was hard.

The second failure is the more expensive one in B2B, and the harder one to see, because everything looks fine. It has a name: the silent veto.

Why usability research does not surface it

Three structural reasons, none of which are a criticism of the method:

  • Tasks are assigned, not chosen. A participant asked to “find the pricing” will find the pricing. A real economic buyer decides whether the pricing gave them a defensible number, which is a different act.
  • Participants are individuals. Usability research samples people. B2B purchases are decided by a committee where one seat can block the rest, and that dynamic does not exist in a single session.
  • The blocking roles rarely participate. The security reviewer and the economic buyer read your page late, alone, from a forwarded link, and are the hardest profiles to recruit for a study — the dark funnel.

Where UserTesting is the right tool and we are not

  • Anything inside the product. Onboarding, workflows, dashboards, mobile. WhyUser reads pages and campaign assets; it does not use software.
  • Task success and time on task. Real measurement of whether people can do the thing.
  • Watching a human be confused. Nothing changes a team’s mind faster than watching someone fail at something the team thought was obvious.
  • Accessibility and interaction defects. Real assistive technology, real hands.
  • Enterprise research programmes. If research is a function in your company, that is a platform decision, not a campaign one.

Running both

They sit at different points and do not compete for the same budget line.

  1. Stress test the asset before spend, to remove decision failures: missing proof, an unanswered role, a broken scent trail between ad and page.
  2. Usability test the experience for the flows that follow the click — signup, trial, onboarding — where a real person’s hands are the only reliable instrument.

A page that no role objects to and nobody can use converts nothing. Neither does a page anyone can use and one role quietly vetoes.

Common questions

What is the difference between WhyUser and UserTesting?

UserTesting is human usability research: real participants complete tasks while narrating, and you learn where people get stuck. WhyUser simulates the B2B buying committee reading a page and reports which role decides against it and what proof was missing. One answers can they use it, the other answers will they buy it.

Can usability testing find a silent veto?

Rarely. Usability studies assign tasks to individuals, and a silent veto is cast by a committee member who reads the page late, alone, from a forwarded link, and never voices the objection. Nothing about that experience is hard to use, so a usability study finds nothing wrong.

Do I need both?

If you run B2B campaigns and also own the product experience, yes, at different points. Stress test the campaign asset before spend to catch decision failures, and usability test the flows after the click where a real person’s hands are the only reliable instrument.

Does WhyUser test inside the product?

No. It reads pages, ads, and emails. Onboarding flows, dashboards, and in-product workflows are usability research territory.
