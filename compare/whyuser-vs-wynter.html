WhyUser vs. Wynter

Both test B2B messaging before you spend. One asks real professionals what they think. The other simulates the whole buying committee and reports which seat vetoes. They answer different questions, and the cheapest sequence runs both.

The short version

Wynter is on-demand B2B market research. You upload a page, ad, or piece of copy, define an audience by title, industry and company size, and verified professionals from Wynter’s panel tell you what they think. You get quantitative scores and qualitative quotes back, typically within a couple of days. The people are real, and the verbatims are quotable.

WhyUser is a pre-launch stress test. It runs the same asset past a simulated buying committee — one agent per role, across several behavioural states — and reports which role abandons, at which section, and what evidence was missing. The buyers are simulated. The evidence they are compiled from is real.

The difference that matters is not human versus synthetic. It is stated preference versus modelled behaviour, and individual responses versus committee dynamics.

Where Wynter is genuinely better

We would rather be straight about this than have you find it out later.

  • Real humans said it. When your CEO has strong opinions about a headline, a verbatim from a verified VP of Engineering ends the argument in a way a simulation does not. This is Wynter’s strongest use and it is not close.
  • Surprise. A panel occasionally tells you something nobody modelled, because a real person brings context no corpus contains.
  • Stated preference on wording. If the question is genuinely “does this phrase land,” ask people.
  • Category and brand perception over time. Awareness and share-of-voice tracking needs humans. A simulation cannot tell you what the market has come to believe.

Where WhyUser does something a panel structurally cannot

A panel samples individuals. Even with perfect targeting, each respondent answers alone. That is the right design for message resonance and the wrong design for one specific and expensive question: which seat kills the deal, and does anything the other seats think matter once it does?

Committee purchases are conjunctive. Every seat has to clear. A page can score well with four roles and still close nothing because the fifth vetoes above the fold. Averaging respondents hides exactly that, which is why WhyUser returns a conflict graph rather than a score.

Two other structural differences:

  • Determinism. Change one section, re-run, and the only thing that moved is the thing you changed. A new panel introduces new people and new noise, so small edits are hard to attribute.
  • Iteration cost. At around 30 minutes and credit-based pricing, you can run eight versions. You would not put eight versions in front of a human panel, and you should not have to.

What neither one does

Neither is an A/B test. Wynter measures what people say; WhyUser models what a role would do. Only live traffic measures what happens. Both exist so that the version you eventually put in front of live traffic is not the first draft.

The sequence that costs least

  1. Stress test first. Run the draft past the simulated committee. Fix the structural failures — the missing security signal, the absent baseline number, the broken scent trail between ad and page. These are cheap to find and embarrassing to pay a panel to discover.
  2. Panel second. Put the survivor in front of real professionals. You are now spending panel budget on the question a panel is uniquely good at — does this land — instead of on defects a simulation catches for a fraction of the cost.
  3. A/B third. Ship the winner into live traffic and let the market settle it.

Running the cheap, deterministic step first is not a knock on the panel. It is how you get more out of it.

How to decide if you only run one

Choose the human panel

Your problem is clarity or resonance. You need a quote to settle an internal argument. You are testing positioning or a category story. You have days rather than minutes, and budget for a panel per test.

Choose the simulation

Your problem is a stalled pipeline with no objection on record. You ship faster than a panel cycle. You need to test the ad and the page together as a pair. You need the same test re-run after every edit without new noise.

Common questions

What is the difference between WhyUser and Wynter?

Wynter collects stated reactions from real B2B professionals on a vetted panel, usually within 12 to 48 hours. WhyUser simulates the whole buying committee as separate agents and reports which role abandons and what evidence was missing, in about 30 minutes. Wynter answers does this land; WhyUser answers which seat kills the deal.

Is WhyUser a replacement for Wynter?

No. They answer different questions and the cheapest sequence runs both: stress test the draft to remove structural failures, then spend panel budget on the resonance question a panel is uniquely good at.

Why can a panel miss a silent veto?

Because a panel samples individuals who each answer alone. Committee purchases are conjunctive, so a page can score well with four roles and still close nothing because a fifth vetoes. Averaging respondents hides exactly that.

Which is faster and cheaper to iterate with?

The simulation, by a wide margin: around 30 minutes per run and credit-based pricing, and deterministic, so re-running after an edit isolates what the edit changed. A new panel introduces new people and new variance.
