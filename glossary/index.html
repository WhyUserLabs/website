The vocabulary of a B2B buying committee.

Deals do not die of bad copy. They die because one seat on the committee needed a piece of proof that was not on the page, and never said so. These are the terms for that, and for the methods that catch it before the budget is spent. Plain definitions, no gate.

Silent veto A committee member kills the deal without ever voicing an objection. Read the definition → Buying committee simulation Every seat on the committee reads the asset separately and decides on its own. Read the definition → Pre-launch stress test The staging step marketing never had: find what breaks while changing it is still free. Read the definition → Conflict graph Who advocates, who hesitates, who vetoes, and which way the influence runs. Read the definition → Scent trail The promise a buyer clicked has to be the first thing they land on. Read the definition → Evidence ledger Predictions sealed before the outcome exists, then graded HIT or MISS. Read the definition → Synthetic persona A buyer compiled from real evidence and run as an agent that decides. Why a glossary

Half of these terms describe a failure that most B2B teams have experienced and have no name for. A deal that stalls with no objection on record. A well-targeted ad pouring qualified clicks into a page that could never convert them. An average conversion score that hides the one role that blocks every deal.

Naming a failure is the first step to testing for it. Each page here gives the definition, why the failure happens, how to spot it after the fact, and how to test for it before launch.

If you want to see the methods rather than read about them: one landing page, judged two ways is interactive and needs no login, and the accuracy page shows how often our predictions have actually been right.
