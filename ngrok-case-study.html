Before WhyUser

Getting to a live test took weeks.

Audience research and copy review both happened before any buyer saw the campaign. Finding the audience took 3–4 weeks. Getting the copy ready took several rounds of review.

01 · Find the audience 3–4 weeks

Research, enrich, group roles, find patterns, validate with Sales.

02 · Get the copy ready Multiple reviews

Draft, review, revise, approve. Then buyers finally saw it.

A lot of people reviewed the campaign before a buyer ever did.

Ground Reality gave ngrok a base committee on day one. It starts when the account is created, with no setup, and builds each simulated buyer from real evidence. From there, Audience Discovery turned each new target into a testable committee within hours. How Audience Discovery works

Three moments before launch

WhyUser surfaced it. ngrok checked it. Then the campaign changed.

Each moment started as a simulated buyer signal. ngrok checked it against Sales, its real inbound or its own proof, and then decided what to change before launch.

  • WhyUser surfaced
  • ngrok checked
  • What changed
01 · Site-to-Site

The missing role pointed back to the page.

WhyUser surfaced
ngrok already knew the four core roles for its Site-to-Site use case, so its existing landing page became the benchmark. Audience Discovery surfaced three of the four. Product Managers were missing.
ngrok checked
The gap raised a question worth checking: what’s missing? Sales had the answer. The Site-to-Site page did not speak to Product Managers.
What changed
ngrok updated the messaging and ran Audience Discovery again. All four core roles surfaced, plus more than 100 related roles to explore.
02 · HealthTech

A new vertical surfaced a role that matched real inbound.

WhyUser surfaced
The first run proved what ngrok already knew. Next, ngrok kept the Site-to-Site use case, changed the lens to HealthTech and ran Audience Discovery again. A strong role signal came back: Clinical Informatics.
ngrok checked
ngrok checked the signal against its real healthcare inbound. It matched. A senior clinical informatics leader was already in that inbound, and had asked about BAAs, the business associate agreements HIPAA requires.
What changed
Discovery became targeting. WhyUser found the roles, ngrok added its inbound and lookalikes, and Sales added buyer knowledge. ngrok built the HealthTech outbound audience in Clay.
03 · Before send

The buyers wanted to verify the HIPAA claim.

Five people at ngrok wrote five outbound ideas. Each one faced the same simulated HealthTech buying committee, built from the new audience, before any email went live.

WhyUser surfaced
The committee flagged a blocker. Its question: “Can I verify the HIPAA claim?”
ngrok checked
The proof existed. ngrok’s HIPAA reports sat in its Trust Center, but the emails did not put them within reach.
What changed
Before launch, ngrok added a direct link to the HIPAA reports in its Trust Center. Buyer feedback became a campaign change.

Who did what

WhyUser brought buyer evidence earlier. ngrok decided what to apply.

WhyUser simulated the buying committee and reported what it found. ngrok supplied the context, checked each signal against reality and made every change.

What WhyUser did and what ngrok did at each stage
StageWhyUser didngrok did
Site-to-Site audience Surfaced three of four core roles and showed Product Managers were missing. Confirmed the gap with Sales, updated the page messaging and ran discovery again.
HealthTech audience Surfaced Clinical Informatics as a strong role signal. Matched it to real healthcare inbound, added lookalikes and Sales knowledge, and built the audience in Clay.
Outbound emails Ran five ideas past one simulated HealthTech buying committee and flagged the HIPAA question. Linked the HIPAA reports in its Trust Center before send.
In market Ran the reworked emails past the committee again. Ran the live test in Apollo, kept two ideas and reworked them when the offer changed.

Every WhyUser signal here is simulated. Where this page says a signal “matched,” ngrok compared it with real data it already had. WhyUser does not claim the live results.

In market

The live test still mattered.

WhyUser changed what ngrok knew before the test began. The market still had the final say.

The five emails went live together in Apollo. While the test was still running, early opens and clicks moved two of the five into the next round.

Then ngrok’s offer changed. ngrok reworked both emails and ran the new versions past the simulated committee again. Next, it applies that feedback and tests again in market.

WhyUser brings buyer evidence earlier. ngrok adds the context and decides what to apply.
