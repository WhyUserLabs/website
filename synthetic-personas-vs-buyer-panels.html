Opposite failure modes

Each method breaks in the other one’s strength.

A panel gives you

  • Real humans. Ours are not, and we will never imply otherwise.
  • Context no corpus holds. A person brings what they lived.
  • Quotable words that settle an internal argument.
  • Its risk: stated preference drifts from real behavior.

A simulation gives you

  • The committee, not forty separate opinions.
  • The handoff, where the deal actually dies.
  • Cheap iteration. Run it eight times, not once.
  • Its risk: thin evidence produces confident nonsense.

Before you buy either

Questions worth asking any vendor.

Ask a persona vendor

Where did the evidence come from, and can you trace a quote to it? Who wrote that source, and what happens when the evidence is thin?

Ask a panel vendor

How is the sample screened, how many respondents actually match my buyer, and what does a second round cost?

Common questions

Answers, briefly.

Are synthetic personas real people?

No, and we will never imply otherwise. What is real is the evidence they are compiled from.

Which should I run first?

The simulation, while fixing things is free. Then spend the panel budget on what survives.

What can a simulation not do?

Bring context no corpus holds, or give you a real human quote to settle an argument.

Do they measure the same thing?

No. A panel measures what people say when asked. A simulation measures what a modeled role does when reading.
