WhyUser vs Wynter

Wynter asks real B2B buyers what they think, one at a time. But what people say is not what they do, and a committee buys B2B software, not one person. WhyUser simulates how the whole committee behaves on your page.

Two gaps a panel cannot close

Said is not done.
One is not a committee.

What people say is not what they do.

A panelist is asked to read your page and rate it, so they read closely. Your buyer is not asked. They skim, hunt for one thing and leave. Opinions gathered this way drift from behavior.

ChampionEnd userBudget owner

No one person buys B2B software.

A champion, an end user, a technical lead, a budget owner and security all have a say. A page can win each of them alone and still stall when the champion cannot pass the case to the budget owner. Panelists answer alone, so a panel cannot see that handoff.

WhyUser simulates behavior by role and mood, scores the handoffs between roles, and your team grades every claim against what really happened: 84% of 473 held up.

What each one is for

Different jobs.
Sometimes both.

Wynter gives you

  • Real people. A panel of 86,000+ verified B2B professionals.
  • Their own words to quote in an internal argument.
  • Targeting by title, seniority, industry, size and region.
  • Its main limit: what people say is not what they do. Panelists read closely because they are asked to. Real buyers skim, get distracted and leave.
  • Also, one person at a time: each respondent answers alone, about 15 per test. A committee buys B2B software.

WhyUser gives you

  • Behavior, not opinions: buyers scan, hunt, stall or leave, in ideal, skeptical and distracted moods.
  • The whole committee, and the handoff between roles where deals stall.
  • Buyers built from your evidence: calls, reviews and buyer questions.
  • Many versions before launch, about 30 minutes each, with ranked fixes.
  • Its limits: simulated buyers, not real people.

Common questions

Answers, briefly.

Isn't a real person better than a simulated one?

For quotes, yes. For what buyers will do, not always. Panelists are asked to read closely and rate the page, so they tell you what they think. Real buyers skim, get distracted and leave. What people say and what they do are often not the same. WhyUser simulates the behavior, by role and mood, and your team grades it against what really happened.

Is WhyUser a Wynter alternative?

For testing many versions before launch, yes. For real human quotes, no. Many teams use both: WhyUser to find and fix problems, then a real panel on the final version.

Which is faster?

WhyUser returns a read in about 30 minutes. Wynter says results arrive in 12 to 48 hours.

Which costs less?

Wynter plans start at $20,000 a year, with single tests from $899. WhyUser plans start at $1,000 a month on an annual plan, and a page test is 20 credits. Prices checked 6 November 2026.

Does Wynter simulate a buying committee?

Not that we found. Wynter targets respondents by title, seniority, industry and size, and each person answers alone. B2B software is bought by a committee, and a page often stalls in the handoff between roles. WhyUser runs five roles together and scores those handoffs.
